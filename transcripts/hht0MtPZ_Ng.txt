Statistically, people with mental illnesses are much more likely to be victims of violence than to be perpetrators of it.  In fact, only 3% of all violent crimes can be attributed to people living with mental illness.  And then there are all the games that take place in a mental hospital or other type of mental health institution designed to instill fear in the players.  It can be quite intimidating to ask for help if you 're struggling with a mental illness, let alone to be admitted to a hospital when you really need it. But the truth is, there's nothing to be ashamed of when you need help or when you ask for it.  And the portrayals in games of mental hospitals as evil or violent places where torture occurs are not fair to the mental health professionals who do everything they can to help those seeking support.  And such portrayals could also discourage people who need that help from seeking it.  If your impressions of mental hospitals are based solely on media portrayals. 
[2m] So, if these are examples of what not to do, what are some ways game designers can pay more attention to get it right?  Well, if you're creating a game that depicts a mental illness, talk to a mental health expert or people who are experts through their lived experience to make sure you do it responsibly.  A great example of this recently was Hellblade: Senua's Sacrifice.  The people who made Hellblade included subject matter experts every step of the way, as well as people who had experienced hearing voices or having hallucinations, and that resulted in a game that was convincing and realistic.  If you want to raise awareness and empathy about a mental health condition, it is not necessary and could even be harmful to try to force your players to experience that condition .  For example, if you're making a game about anxiety, don't try to force your players to have an anxiety attack in order for them to understand it.  That doesn't mean games should never have difficult themes.  It simply means that you should always keep the mental health of your players in mind and not do anything that could cause them a breakdown.  Having a 
[3m] content warning like the one that appears at the beginning of Depression Quest helps, as does offering players the option to skip certain content without affecting the rest of their game, something that the developers of the game Anamorphine have implemented.  But again, including subject matter experts during the design process is probably the most helpful thing you can do.  None of this means you shouldn't include characters who deal with mental health conditions, but don't make that illness the only thing that defines them .  Make that person someone multifaceted and complex for whom mental illness is only one part of their life.  I know this example isn't from a game, but Forrest Gump is an example of a character who is much more than the mental struggles he faces.  And there are ways in which anyone can identify with him. Furthermore, it is important to note that not all villains have mental illnesses.  Perhaps they are just people who , due to a few bad decisions, circumstances, or experiences, pursue their goals in a way that is different from most, like Killmonger in Black Panther.  But 
[4m] when you portray a character with a mental illness, don't make a single symptom the sole indicator of that illness.  For example, hallucinations as the main feature of schizophrenia. Most mental health conditions have several indicator symptoms, and using only one reduces the illness to a cliché and a stereotype instead of getting to the heart of what people are facing.  Not to mention that mental illness can manifest differently in each person, and no two cases are exactly alike.  A person with schizophrenia might have hallucinations, of course, but not all patients with that condition will experience them.  In addition, characters with mental illnesses can be represented in a simple way, such as showing a character taking their medication or mentioning that they visited a therapist.  It is perfectly fine to show the mundane everyday reality of mental illness, which could help combat the stigma.  Always do good when you can, but at least don't do harm.  And finally, there is certain terminology you should be aware of when talking about mental illness. We always want to focus on the 
[5m] human being and not on the condition, leaving the person in the background.  Therefore , using "person with mental illness" is preferable to "mentally ill person".  "Demented" or "insane" is a legal term used to establish whether someone can be considered competent to stand trial for a crime.  Actually, it's not a term that's used at all in mental health care. Then there's the word "crazy". Situations and circumstances like the weather or traffic may be called crazy, but when you call a person crazy, it's not as considerate as we should be regarding mental health.  In fact, there's a pretty good list of words you should avoid if you want to be more sensitive about it .  So those are some ways to design more thoughtfully when you think about how to represent and talk about mental illness in games.
