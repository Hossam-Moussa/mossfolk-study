In today's edition of Card Game Design, we'll talk about the Buddyfight value system and how "power creep " (increasing card strength) was introduced in each series of the game.  When the game first came out, the Future Card Buddyfight value system was like this.  One card is equal to two life units, two gauge units, two critical hits, 6000 strength, 6000 defense, or one attack.  Rather than dissect where this equation came from, I'll direct you to Buddyfight Theory's excellent article 
[1m] on the subject.  They explained everything perfectly, so I would only be repeating myself if I delved into it today.  Instead, we'll focus on how Buddyfight implemented " power creep" while more or less adhering to the original value system.  Early in the game and even in Buddyfight 100, the value system was strictly enforced, and there was almost no "power creep". We see changes in Buddyfight Triple D due to increased card flexibility.  Triple D not only introduced cards used in many worlds, but also added significantly more selection effects on cards.  This gave a single card multiple uses, occupying one slot but fulfilling two roles, allowing for more flexibility in deck building.  In Buddyfight X, the "Invigorating Breath" condition has become ubiquitous.    The "Invigorating Breath" condition 
[2m] applies to the Danger World— Invigorating Breath spell card.  This card allowed you to gain four life points for the price of one card from your hand.  According to our value system, that would be a net "plus one."  So where does the extra price come from?  Invigorating Breath requires the player to have an item equipped before the card can be played. Thus, Invigorating Breath demonstrates a design approach according to which for each card invested in a skill, you can get an additional " plus one".  Buddyfight X took this to the extreme : almost every card had an Invigorating Breath-style initial action. Finally, moving on to Buddyfight Ace. Buddyfight Ace's contribution to power creep is the removal of the "once per turn" restriction .  Skills that previously clearly had a " once per turn" limit, such as the famous layer effect, now do not. This leads to 
[3m] Yu-Gi-Oh!-style results.  The style of moves, which can last tens of minutes, has made loyalty almost an integral element.  Although monster stats have been steadily increasing, this increase has been quite slow compared to other games, and that's a good thing. Overall, I like how Buddyfight handled power creep and card design, as the only Buddyfight series I didn't like was Ace. Thank you for watching, liking, and subscribing.
