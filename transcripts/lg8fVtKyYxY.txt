It's actually kind of hard to pin down choice because, given free will, it's easy to call everything one does a choice. This tautology isn't very helpful. Calculations are decisions based solely on reason, with a clear correct answer. These sort of mental tasks can be as straightforward as a math problem. Say, I offered to sell you a game for 60 bucks, and your buddy offers to sell you the exact same game for 40 bucks. In this case, deciding which offer to take is a calculation. A slightly more complicated version is deciding not to place your hand on a hot stove. In this case, you know what the result will be if you do so, burning. You know that burning causes you pain, you know that pain is bad, and you probably can't think of anything that putting your hand on the stove would accomplish that could possibly outweigh the consequence of searing pain. Therefore, not putting your hand on the stove clearly the correct decision. Calculation, logical. So, calculations involve decisions, but they're not choices. This is a key point to remember when talking about choice in games. So, what is choice? Choice is overcoming internal conflict. Without conflict, 
[2m] there is no choice, only decisions. And it is this very fact, this overcoming of internal conflict, that makes choice so engaging. Now, so far this has all been pretty highbrow, so let's start digging into the important stuff. How do we use this in games? Super Mario Brothers. Super Mario Brothers uses choice brilliantly. It sets up a long-term goal, rescue this, defeat that, and a short-term goal, stay alive. It then goes about finding ways to set up situations where these two goals are in opposition. When you hit a block and a mushroom pops out, that mushroom starts traveling to the right towards the unknown. Getting the mushroom will make you big, allowing you to take two hits before you die, and thus making it more likely that you'll be able to accomplish your long-term goal, save this, defeat that. This type of choice is called an incomplete information problem because the player
