One of the reasons we establish the status quo is so we can see what is the norm, which will then be broken by the next part of the first act —the routine killer.    A routine killer is an event that disrupts the status quo for the protagonist. It has many different names, so be careful, but the most common ones I've come across are routine killer and triggering incident.  It can be anything you want.  It doesn't have to be a big event, it just has to be weird and wonderful enough to disrupt the main character's status quo .  However, a routine killer usually needs to have three characteristics to work properly. It should be unexpected and come like a bolt from the blue.  It should be sudden so that the protagonist cannot plan actions around it, and it should be difficult to undo or fix so that the character is forced to deal 
[4m] with it and cannot simply write it off in the next scene. The established norm is to consider “ routine killer” and “call to adventure” to be one and the same.  I prefer to divide them into two separate narrative concepts.  They can be one and the same if it suits the story, but sometimes you can keep the audience in suspense and develop the events a little before overwhelming them with a " call to adventure."    A call to adventure is a new turn of events that prompts the protagonist to respond and embark on a narrative journey.  This is where we show the consumer what the main character will be doing at the beginning of the second act. This is a good time to tell the audience what the piece will be about, even if the character himself doesn't yet grasp the full scope.  Obi-Wan invites Luke to go with him to Alderaan.
