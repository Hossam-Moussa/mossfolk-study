Digital Collectible Card Games or Digital&nbsp; CCG for short, is an extremely popular genre.&nbsp;&nbsp; Despite having a long story, the genre explosion&nbsp; started with Hearthstone in 2014 and since then,&nbsp;&nbsp; as it happens with any popular genre, many games&nbsp; launched trying to harness the popularity of it.&nbsp;&nbsp; Sadly, due to lack of innovation in both theme&nbsp; and mechanics, many of these games have died&nbsp;&nbsp; in oblivion, while most of the remaining and&nbsp; newly launched titles are being constantly&nbsp;&nbsp; compared to Hearthstone as a benchmark of the&nbsp; genre, thus decreasing the interest of players.&nbsp;&nbsp; In this video, we are going to analyze&nbsp; the main parts and systems of Hearthstone,&nbsp;&nbsp; while also trying to define guidelines to innovate&nbsp;&nbsp; in a genre that appears to be stagnant trying to&nbsp; replicate a single game. Collectible Cards Games have been around since&nbsp; 1993, with the introduction of the business and&nbsp;&nbsp; design model of Magic: The Gathering. It was the&nbsp; first card game to introduce the concept of main&nbsp;&nbsp; decks and booster packs, in which players have the&nbsp; option to buy new cards to build better decks. It&nbsp;is not that “optional” though, because newly released&nbsp; cards tend to be more powerful than old ones,&nbsp;&nbsp; a characteristic called “power creep”. You&nbsp; can find more on the Extra Credits video&nbsp;&nbsp; that will appear in the card. Now, the change from analog to digital CCG&nbsp;&nbsp; brought many changes in the mechanics that would&nbsp; be difficult to implement in an analog card game,&nbsp;&nbsp; for example, keeping track of the cards played in&nbsp; a specific turn or playing with bigger numbers.&nbsp;&nbsp; 
[2m] Also, the actions are executed automatically,&nbsp; thus players don’t need to enforce the rules&nbsp;&nbsp; themselves, which in turn diminishes&nbsp; the cognitive load on the player's mind.&nbsp;&nbsp; With the new digital era, most CCG started&nbsp; to fade, and switching to digital was the&nbsp;&nbsp; only option for many of them, while&nbsp; new games were created to fill the gap&nbsp;&nbsp; in the market. That’s where&nbsp; Blizzard’s Hearthstone appeared.&nbsp; Hearthstone is a free-to-play game developed and&nbsp; published by Blizzard. It was the first digital&nbsp;&nbsp; CCG developed by Blizzard and as of today,&nbsp; is the benchmark for other digital CCG games.&nbsp;&nbsp; Hearthstone introduced many improvements to the&nbsp; CCG formula that are directed towards getting rid&nbsp;&nbsp; of gameplay steps, in order to speed up the game&nbsp; and ease the cognitive load of players. One of the&nbsp;&nbsp; 
[3m] main improvements to this formula was not allowing&nbsp; players to take actions in the opponent's turn. It&nbsp;&nbsp; is a completely different paradigm than Magic: The&nbsp; gathering, in which, for example, players choose&nbsp;&nbsp; which character to block in the opponent’s turn. Hearthstone simplified tremendously&nbsp;&nbsp; the CCG core gameplay loop, creating a&nbsp; kind of template for many games after it,&nbsp;&nbsp; especially for card battler games. The core&nbsp; gameplay loop consists of the following:&nbsp;&nbsp; First, the player starts a new turn with a hand of&nbsp; cards. The player plays a card if it's allowed by&nbsp;&nbsp; the rules. Then use the cards to attack and the&nbsp; turns end, starting the same loop for the enemy.&nbsp;&nbsp; Then a new turn starts, adding a gem that&nbsp; allows the player to play more powerful cards.&nbsp;&nbsp; 
[4m] As you see, the enemy can’t interact and&nbsp; it has no way to disrupt the player’s turn.&nbsp; Besides, each turn the relative power of the cards&nbsp;&nbsp; that the player can play increases, bringing&nbsp; the game to an end before it feels too long.&nbsp;&nbsp; This obviously reduces the player’s&nbsp; agency and possibility of surprise,&nbsp;&nbsp; because when the enemy finishes playing his or her&nbsp; cards, there is not much that can be done. A way&nbsp;&nbsp; to solve this is to add agency in other parts of&nbsp; the game, like allowing players to discard some of&nbsp;&nbsp; their cards at the start of the game or “juicy”&nbsp; interactions, like pressing in any part of the&nbsp;&nbsp; field to watch dust particles rise. It’s not much,&nbsp; but it’s enough to keep players thinking that they&nbsp;&nbsp; are having a fair play against the enemy and&nbsp; keep them entertained when it’s not their turn.&nbsp; 
[5m] Hearthstone is composed of 4 types of&nbsp; cards: minion, spell, weapon and hero cards.&nbsp;&nbsp; Most games follow a similar hierarchy of cards.&nbsp; There are cards that can be played that have an&nbsp;&nbsp; attack and defense, that is deducted automatically&nbsp; every time the card attacks or is attacked. There&nbsp;are cards that have an immediate unique action&nbsp; and there are other cards that can be equipped&nbsp;&nbsp; to a card already played to enhance their power.&nbsp; What is interesting, is that this set of mechanics&nbsp;&nbsp; can be applied to any kind of aesthetic, like&nbsp; figurines or even to a JRPG turn-based mechanic.&nbsp;&nbsp; This is why there is an increasing trend to&nbsp; include card-like mechanics in other contexts,&nbsp;&nbsp; like Slay the Spire, Trials of Fire and&nbsp; many more have done. Card battlers consist of&nbsp;&nbsp; 
[6m] such robust systems, that they can be applied in&nbsp; many contexts retaining the core gameplay loop&nbsp;&nbsp; and interactions. Heck, it’s so versatile that you&nbsp; can put any theme on top of it and it would work&nbsp;&nbsp; exactly the same, it doesn’t matter if it’s&nbsp; medieval, fantasy, animé or plants vs zombies.&nbsp; Now, to prove my point, I want to&nbsp; briefly show you three games that&nbsp;&nbsp; are somewhat different to Hearthstone,&nbsp; some more successfully far than others.&nbsp; Causa is a game developed by Niebla Games,&nbsp; released this year. The game gives an&nbsp;&nbsp; interesting overturn to the mechanic of discard&nbsp; at the start of the match. Powerful cards&nbsp;&nbsp; remain in the player’s hand, who in turn&nbsp; has the option to sacrifice them to obtain&nbsp;&nbsp; “Causa” , the currency equivalent to gems in&nbsp; Hearthstone, so players generate their own gems.&nbsp;&nbsp; 
[7m] Despite adding this new layer of&nbsp; difficulty, the main loop doesn’t&nbsp;&nbsp; change too much and reading the Steam comments,&nbsp; many players compare the game with Hearthstone,&nbsp;&nbsp; some in a good way, some don’t. It’s a fun&nbsp; game with an interesting turn in that mechanic.&nbsp; KARDS is World War II themed-game developed by&nbsp; 1939 games, also released this year. The game has&nbsp;&nbsp; the typical cards with attack, defense and some&nbsp; simple powers triggered in different situations.&nbsp;&nbsp; The game allows the player to change some cards&nbsp; at the start of the game like Hearthstone. One&nbsp;interesting innovation is the adding of&nbsp; a physical space called the frontline, an&nbsp;&nbsp; intermediate line that is the main way to attack&nbsp; the enemy’s base and win the game. The difference&nbsp;&nbsp; with other games that use physical space is that&nbsp; the frontline is very significant to the game,&nbsp;&nbsp; 
[8m] because the player that controls the frontline&nbsp; is the only one in a position to deliver a great&nbsp;&nbsp; amount of damage to the enemy, so it’s important&nbsp; to know when to move the troops to the frontline.&nbsp;&nbsp; A bad movement can ruin the&nbsp; whole match for a player though.&nbsp; Finally, Gwent is the well&nbsp; known collectible card game&nbsp;&nbsp; born from the Witcher world. This game deviates&nbsp; completely from Hearthstone’s or Magic’s formula.&nbsp;&nbsp; Gwent is played in three rounds and a single set&nbsp; of at least 22 cards. One of the most important&nbsp;&nbsp; differences of this game is&nbsp; that all cards are important,&nbsp;&nbsp; because literally, all cards can be played in a&nbsp; match. This is important, because it reduces the&nbsp;&nbsp; total number of cards in a single deck, and&nbsp; also, gives more meaning to the link between&nbsp;&nbsp; deck-building and gameplay. Also it is important&nbsp; to note that Gwent is not a card battler.&nbsp; 
[9m] The natural question at this point&nbsp; is… Why did I make this video?&nbsp;&nbsp; What is the point that I’m trying to make? Well,&nbsp; to be perfectly clear, I think that digital CCG&nbsp;&nbsp; lack innovation, both in theme and mechanics. Most&nbsp; CCG, as they are card battler games, rely heavily&nbsp;&nbsp; on variation of medieval fantasy, probably a&nbsp; consequence of the dominance of Hearthstone,&nbsp;&nbsp; and probably the consequence of the alleged target&nbsp; public. There are games that explore other themes,&nbsp;&nbsp; like KARDS, but the example of successful games&nbsp; are few. On the mechanic side, most collectible&nbsp;&nbsp; card games are card battler games and rely heavily&nbsp; on the same parts and mechanics as Hearthstone&nbsp;&nbsp; or in some cases as Magic: The Gathering. The&nbsp; innovation comes little by little and is usually&nbsp;&nbsp; 
[10m] in systems that doesn’t influence the experience&nbsp; in a disruptive way, for example, changing how the&nbsp;&nbsp; player deal with powerful cards that require more&nbsp; gems to be played or in how the gameplay area is&nbsp;&nbsp; distributed, adding a sense of physicality&nbsp; that is rarely satisfactory to the player,&nbsp;&nbsp; because it ended up more like a burden to players’&nbsp; turn rather than a highly strategic decision. I think that if digital CCG designers start to&nbsp; look at other genres besides the card battlers,&nbsp;&nbsp; they could add many ideas to the genre like&nbsp; point systems or a sense of&nbsp;&nbsp; physicality that is meaningful and&nbsp; fun. Also, it would be useful to start looking&nbsp;&nbsp; to other themes besides the medieval fantasy,&nbsp; to give a fresh air to a genre that is mostly&nbsp;&nbsp; surviving due to a niche of players that consume&nbsp; new games in order to test if this is, finally,&nbsp;&nbsp;
