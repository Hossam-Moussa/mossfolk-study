Companies realized long ago that if they could make their store part of your daily routine, they would get much more money from you and would have to spend much less money on advertising .  Are you familiar with those "buy 10 get 1 free" cards they give you at local coffee shops?  They're not just an attempt to lure you in with a functional 9% discount.  In reality, they are an attempt to keep you coming back until going to their store becomes a regular thing.  And that's exactly what these energy systems want you to do.  They want you to get into the habit of playing their games every day. These systems have been appearing in games for a while, but they first became popular with World of Warcraft.  The rest experience system in World of Warcraft is a habituation system.  It's powerful because one of the main motivators for many people who play WoW is the strange human desire for efficiency. In any MMO, you'll find a subset of players who seek to maximize the efficiency with which they can progress in the game.  But in almost all human beings, when presented with clear mathematical efficiencies , there is almost a compulsion to take the most efficient path. Without this type of habituation, their games would simply fail.  But they incorporated an additional element.  Instead of simply penalizing you for playing more than a certain amount in a short period of time, they actually prevented you from doing so mechanically.  The reason for this is twofold.
