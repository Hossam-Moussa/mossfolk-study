
[0m] We have spent a lot of time talking about game graphics on the channel. I think it’s time to move towards game design. This new video series will not talk about programming, or at least not at first. Why? simply because design and programming are 2 different forms of languages, which we have to learn separately before we can make them meet. At the core of a game, and I’m not only talking about video games, there is a number of rules, systems, one or several goals, and a universe that structure the player’s experience. Thinking about the mechanics or the game systems is at the heart of the work of a game designer. Here’s how the upcoming videos will work: they are going to focus on a small system and break it down. I’m thinking of cutting every topic in 2 parts. The first video will be an analysis of a few reference games, and the one after it will be dedicated to applying what we learned, still from a game design standpoint. From time to time, I would also like to cover specific questions in this series. 
[1m] The idea is to explain in greater details why game design is important, what a game engine is, etc. the next video will actually be dedicated to game jams, following on the recent ludum dare. The goal is to help us better understand how a game is made, bit by bit. But I’m also looking to share my game design knowledge with you, and to introduce some of you to the process of crafting a game, which is neither abstract nor linear. These videos are also meant to allow those who code among you to learn concepts that you can apply to your own projects, regardless of the engine you’re using. Along with every theme, you will get an example prototype made with meta2d, an open source HTML5 game engine. Meta is lightweight and easy to use. It offers just the right amount of functionality to make all sorts of 2d games. Any developer with a little experience in JavaScript should be able to read scripts 
[2m] made with meta and understand them, without having ever used the engine before. I’m also collaborating with the engine’s author, Arthur Šefers. Thus I know where the engine is going, and what its philosophy is. In any case, it’s not too important, as this series isn’t about game programming: it’s only about design. If you’re interested in exploring game design with me, I invite you to come back next week to embark on a new journey, on this channel. Thank you kindly for watching! Be creative, have fun… until next time!
