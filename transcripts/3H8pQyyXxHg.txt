[1m] Flow has a simple progression that we're going to be working through. We want to present the player with a challenge. The challenge will put the player into a flow state and then we want to keep them in that flow state. These three steps revolve around the flow state, so what is that? Flow is the idea of giving a challenge to an individual and through that person engaging with that challenge they reach a flow state. This is what happens when you start playing a game and then you stop to look outside and it's dark and you realize that you've been playing for six hours. Flow states are sometimes described as being "in the zone." It is a state of hyper focus where the individual is fully engaged in the moment. Awareness of self and surroundings fall away and each action flows from one to the next, almost naturally. The player is completely enthralled with the game and they are not paying attention to anything else. We use flow theory to help create a game that is engaging to the player and being in a flow state can be seen as being at peak engagement with an activity. We want to present the player with a challenge that will get them into a flow state ... but what sort of challenge? In order to work out a challenge we need to consider how skilled the player is. The idea is to give the player a challenge that requires slightly more skill than the player already has. At the start of the game however the player will have little to no skill in operating the systems of the game. In order to illustrate these two ideas we use what is called a flow graph. A flow graph is a chart that maps player skill on the x-axis and challenge along the y-axis. When players start the game they have a little skill or understanding.
