So, let's use this time to add some new thoughts to something we discussed long, long ago. What makes an effective horror protagonist? Horror in art, whether it be in games, books, television, or movies, is an empathetic experience. We must, in some way, feel the terror that the characters are feeling in order for horror to be effective. Without this, we're simply left with an exposition of gore or an exercise in sadism, neither of which is, at its heart, truly horror. Therefore, 
[1m] we need a vessel to experience that horror through, a protagonist we can empathize with, believe in, and even immerse ourselves in when playing, rather than more passively experiencing horror. So, how do we do this? Well, first, the most obvious point, the more that the protagonist is like us, the more we can empathize with them. This is why characters like Ripley from Aliens or Harry Mason from Silent Hill work so well. Even in extraordinary circumstances, they're still just ordinary people with concerns we can understand. This is why the horror that Sarah Connor feels is more present for many of us than, say, Dutch pushing all those CIA pencils in Predator.
