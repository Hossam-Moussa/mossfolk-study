There were a lot of problems understanding the rules of the game because they weren't fully developed when the cards were created. Everyone still had fun, but it was clear that the game lacked structure. The situation was complicated by the anime , which entrenched erroneous rules that never applied to the physical card game.  It was during one of the most popular story arcs, the Battle City arc, that the game gained real structure.  This introduced sacrificing lower- 
[20m] level monsters to summon higher- level monsters.  This showed that traps must first be placed on the field, and cannot be played directly from the hand.  But one problem remained— the confusion surrounding the card “Mystical Space Typhoon.”    The card reads: " Choose one spell or trap on the field."    " Destroy the selected target." Many players used this card in an attempt to counter spells and stop their opponent halfway.  However, dueling experts point out a major difference: "Mystical Space Typhoon" only destroys the map.  It does not cancel the card effect. The cancellation of the effect would be clearly stated in the card text, like in "Dark Bribe".  "Mystical Space Typhoon" is specifically designed to destroy spells or traps that remain on the field, known as continuous spells.  One problem that many card games face is counterfeit cards.  To combat this, a special hologram 
[21m] , known as the "Eye of Anubis", is printed on each Yu-Gi-Oh card.  This embossing trademarked by Upper Deck, was absent from some of the earliest Yu-Gi-Oh sets.  And it's quite difficult to understand which cards were fakes and which weren't .  This hologram is printed directly on the card, so anyone who wants to verify authenticity will immediately see if the hologram is just a sticker applied. These holograms also help distinguish between first edition cards and reissued or, as collectors call them, unlimited cards.    The first edition card will have a gold hologram, while the unlimited reissue will have a silver hologram. This particular kind of hologram is used stylistically in some other card games, but for Yu-Gi-Oh cards it is integral.  There is a small group of people 
[22m] who create original maps, or “Ora” for short. At first, it looked like an attempt to create fake Yu-Gi-Oh cards.  However, it later became clear that this was not the case.  The main difference between fakes and Ora is that Ora never tries to convince someone that it is a real card.  Ora is completely dedicated to experimenting with the possibilities of Yu-Gi-Oh as a game and with card design.  Often people buy original cards when the player is on a budget and cannot afford the real version of the card.  Therefore, they use the original map as a proxy.
