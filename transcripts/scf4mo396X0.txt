In fact, one of the harder goals and the main goal of a game designer is to find the fun. Game design is a series of knobs, dials, and sliders, all which can be adjusted and manipulated to maximize the player experience. So, let's take a look at a recent example I personally have had to work with in my own game, Mimic. In the game, players have the ability to indirectly combat other players by casting ballots and voting for which other players should be targeted by their AI enemies. This presents a problem to solve, though. We can't interrupt play. The game is a horror game, so any interruption or pausing of the action will mean a lot of the tension we have built up with the player. So, how do we get the player to engage in a system that is designed to be optional, but feel compelled to use it? So I took inspiration from real life and looked for similar problems that have

Secondly, I adjusted the threat the AI poses so the players will feel more of a benefit from taking advantage of the system.
