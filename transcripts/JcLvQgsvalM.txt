A weenie is a point of interest in level design to draw the players attention and focus. Weenies originated from the world of theme-park design. Often when learning about weenies we hear about how they are used in disney world in order to draw people to different places in the park and allow them to know where they are. An example of this is the castle at Disney World that draws people into the center of the park and can always be used for orientation in order to know roughly where you are located or how to get back to the center of the park. Often, but not always, a weenie will be something positioned high up or something that is inherently tall like a tower or a castle spire so that it can be seen from many places. But weenies can also just be interesting things around the level or things that are unnaturally large that stand out. Weenies are used for several purposes. The first is to draw attention. 
[1m] Directing the player is something that we are constantly doing. Weenies can be used to grab the players attention in a level to help direct them through the level or to a place of interest that we want them to explore. Weenies can also be used to allow the player to orient themselves in the landscape. To prevent the player getting turned around and then progressing away from their goal, a weenie can be used to allow the player to get their bearings again. This is helpful when the player might get distracted or when we offer side quests and activities that will distract the player from their path. After completion the player can re-orient themselves and continue on their way. An example of this might be the Rock of Ravatough or these rock formations in Final Fantasy XV. You can quickly get your bearings after being in the woods or the mass fusion building in Fallout 4 which can be seen for miles away and is more or less the center of the map. Weenies are also used as points of interest to work towards. For more open games where the player may find themselves directing their own play, weenies offer starting points for the players to head towards. This is largely the case in Breath of the Wild which we will revisit in a minute or in Elder

Finally the least of these cases, weenies can be used to show progression or to somewhat foreshadow. Breath of the Wild attempts to do almost all of these things and expand the use of weenies. It does this through the use of triangles in the level design. Large portions of

This would pique their interest but in order to get to the weenie they would need to go past a structure that was a large triangle that slightly obscures it from view. This left the player with a choice: either climb over the triangle which is usually a mountain or go around it. If the player chooses to go over it, the mountain is the new challenge and we head out with our goal. After reaching the top we can get our bearings and continue. If the player chooses to go around it, the designers made sure that there were plenty of interesting things for the player to get distracted by. They wanted the player to go off on tangents and each time they returned to their original path they could reorient to the weenie that was the original point of interest that they were progressing towards and off they go.
