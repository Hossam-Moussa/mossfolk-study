We started lashing out at other players for hindering our fun, calling their method of play incorrect, claiming that we know the right way to do it, so they should let us show them how it's done.  The problem with this, of course, is that the person we are trying to correct doesn't see it that way.  They have their own way of enjoying the game, and suddenly it seems like we're going over it. Playing for your audience can partially solve this problem.  What this means is finding like-minded people who enjoy playing the same way you do.
