Third, dependence on luck.    The 
[2m] Vanguard Zero uses a trigger system.  In this system, when your main unit attacks, you reveal one or two cards from the top of your deck.  If these cards have a trigger icon, one of your units is granted 5000 power, as well as another effect. When a player takes damage, a trigger check is also applied. The game, to put it mildly, is overly dependent on these triggers. One trigger can completely affect the outcome of a game, especially on defense. Since a player not participating in a turn cannot change the outcome of a battle, winning or losing is tied to whether you get a damage trigger.  Then, if your opponent gets a damage trigger as the attacker, you will need it when your Vanguard attacks to properly execute the move as planned.  Essentially, you either get triggers or you lose.  The system creates a sense in players that the game depends on luck, which it is, and not on player intervention or skill, which it is not.
